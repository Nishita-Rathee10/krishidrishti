How we built it

This page explains where the numbers come from and how we checked them. We tested every model against a simple rule, and we say so where the simple rule did as well.

Our way of working

  • We decided how a model would be judged before looking at its results.
  • Every model had to beat a simple rule, such as "same as last year" or "the usual pattern". If it did not, we report that.
  • Data from the final test years was looked at once. The rain page says plainly where we looked twice.
  • Every chart has a table version, and every page lists what the numbers cannot tell you.

Rain

We compared the usual pattern with a seasonal time-series model (SARIMA). Each year, every method got the records up to the year before and guessed all 12 months. We chose methods on 1971 to 2005 and looked at 2006 to 2015.

Crops that may suit a district

A crop is major in a district when it covers at least 2% of that season's area. We averaged a logistic regression and a random forest on rain, temperature and humidity. Each district was judged by a model that never saw it.

What we left out, and why

  • Fertilizer advice. A fair tool needs soil test results for each farm or block. We do not have them in a usable form, and a guess from climate alone would mislead.
  • Market prices. The price files we found were either very messy or too short to test a forecast fairly, so we do not forecast prices.
  • Water, soil and seed. Canals, tube wells, soil type and seed variety shape a farm's yield. None of them is in our data.
  • The future. Our records end in 2015 for rain and 2019 for crops. The yield model does not see a year's weather, so it cannot say anything useful about a year to come.
