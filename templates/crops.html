All districts

Crops

A crop counts as major in a district when it covers at least 2% of the area recorded for its season. Our model learns what the rain, temperature and humidity are like where a crop is major, then checks which other districts look similar.

It sees climate only. It does not see water, soil, seed or market prices, which matter just as much to a farmer.

Crops we show

We show a crop only when it passed three checks in our own test: it is major in at least 5 districts and missing in at least 5, and the model ranked the right district higher at least 70 times out of 100.

What to keep in mind

  • These are links in the data, not causes. A district may grow little of a crop because of water, soil, markets or habit, and not because of its climate.
  • Crop shares are averages for 2010 to 2019. Temperatures come from a NASA model for 2001 to 2025. Rain is the average of 1986 to 2015.
  • We cover {{ c.districts }} districts. For a crop that is major in only a few of them, a test score can swing a lot.
  • A suggestion is a place to look further. It is not advice to change what you grow.
